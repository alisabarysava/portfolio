Trip Request Page Redesign

Company iTechArt
Year 2026
Domain Traveltech
Role Product Designer

Intro

The trip request details page is part of an internal product over 5 years old, whose layout had barely changed while accumulating increasingly complex functionality and bulky data blocks. As a result, key content ended up far down the scroll, making it hard to get a full picture of the request at a glance — and the chat, a critical communication channel during a trip, sat at the very bottom of the page, below every other section.

Approach

The product owner asked to reduce scroll, keep part of the content permanently visible, make the rest more compact, and align the layout with a similar internal product for consistency.

I restructured the page into three persistent columns instead of one long central feed: participants and traveler info on the left, request details and budget in the center, chat and history fixed on the right for the full height of the screen. Heavy data blocks (expense categories, total expenses breakdown, advance payments, potential reimbursement) moved from inline display into slide-in side panels triggered on click — the summary stays visible at all times, while details open on demand.

Key Decisions

  1. Chat moved into a persistent column. This directly addressed the core pain point — chat used to end up at the bottom of a multi-screen page, despite being used most actively during the trip. Now it's visible regardless of what's happening in the center column, with no scrolling required at all.
  2. Progressive disclosure for heavy data blocks. Instead of keeping Expense categories, Total expenses, Advance payments, and Potential reimbursement fully expanded on the page, I kept only the key figure inline and moved full detail into a side panel opened on click. This sharply reduced the page's baseline height without removing a single data point — it's just one click away instead of always taking up space.
  3. Reorganized the top info block into tabs. Purpose & outcome and Project info, previously separate blocks on the page, became tabs alongside Request info — bringing the structure closer to the sibling product's layout, as requested, while removing another vertical block from the main flow.

Result

According to feedback from the team, the chat became noticeably easier to use — it's now always visible and no longer requires scrolling to the bottom of the page. Regular travelers noted that navigating documents became easier thanks to the added thumbnails, making it visually simpler to tell documents apart without opening each one individually.
